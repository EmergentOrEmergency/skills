# Discovery through voice and inner conflict

Read when promoting introspective, literary, or first-person fiction whose attraction depends on subjective experience.

## Find the promise in the text

Extract the concrete situation, what the narrator wants now, what they avoid or misread, pressure on that avoidance, and the experience offered to readers. Describe only what the material supports.

First person does not imply autobiography. Keep narrator, character, and author distinct. Never market invented events as testimony or assign psychological diagnoses unsupported by the work.

Build positioning from the actual tension, such as belonging versus self-protection or loyalty versus independence. Preserve ambiguity rather than reducing the work to a self-help lesson or advertising a revelation it leaves open.

## Select excerpts

Choose a passage where voice and action interact: a small decision, words contradicted by behavior, a charged object, or an encounter whose significance is partly unadmitted. Check orientation, characteristic rhythm/perception/humor, spoiler boundaries, standalone intelligibility, and fidelity after cuts.

An interior passage can work without overt action when its voice carries tension. Do not replace quiet work with its most sensational incident by default. Prefer contiguous excerpts; identify omissions/adaptations instead of presenting a collage as verbatim prose.

## Try distinct presentations

Offer a small set grounded in the same text:

1. Situation-led: brief setup plus representative passage.
2. Voice-led: a genuine line followed by only the orientation needed to continue.
3. Listening-led: a recording brief with the preferred performer, a passage with a coherent spoken arc, and delivery notes grounded in its rhythm.

For audio, distinguish narrator from author in the introduction. Provide a transcript and full-story destination. Prepare a recording brief if tools/recordings are absent; never claim audio exists. Do not clone a person's voice without their authorization.

Match imagery and copy to tone and spoiler boundaries. Avoid misleading thriller packaging for an intimate story.

## Illustrative positioning

The following is invented, not a quotation from any user's work:

> He returns to empty his father's house. Each day, he finds another reason not to open one drawer.

It shows how an everyday action can carry an emotional question. Do not import the parent, drawer, loss, or secret unless present in the real text. A comic, joyful, or ambivalent narrator deserves an equally specific promise without invented trauma.

## Measure resonance

Choose the primary action before release: start the full text, subscribe for future work, or explore an existing story. Replies may reveal what resonates but are not a representative survey.

For a standalone story, a future-work invitation is enough; do not invent a sequel. Compare presentations on comparable audiences and exposure windows. Treat views, email opens, and audio plays as intermediate signals unless actual reading/return can be observed.
