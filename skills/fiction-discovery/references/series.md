# Collections, linked series, and serial releases

Read when choosing entry stories, allocating publication, or arranging recurring releases.

## Identify the reading contract

| Format | Entry-point decision | Release/navigation decision |
|---|---|---|
| Independent stories | Select a strong, accessible story representative of the collection. | Offer a thematic/tonal next choice; do not invent a mandatory sequence. |
| Linked stories | Choose a story requiring little prior knowledge; identify dependencies. | Provide recommended order and brief orientation when needed. |
| One serialized narrative | Begin at the intended starting point unless the author requests otherwise. | Number installments and maintain previous/next links and a series index. |

Name the actual connection between stories: question, setting, recurring character, or style. Do not invent a shared universe or causal sequence.

## Select the gateway

Compare actual candidates on standalone intelligibility, setup burden, distinctive voice/situation, representativeness, emotional satisfaction, spoilers, dependencies, and availability for release. Explain the strongest choice and its main tradeoff. A short entry is useful only if it represents the work; a later story should not be a gateway if it reveals an earlier resolution.

## Allocate the material

Classify each story as `FULL_RELEASE`, `EXCERPT`, `RESERVED`, or `UNDECIDED`, with the reason. These editorial decisions are separate from rights status and execution state.

A complete free story can demonstrate quality while the collection offers an actual benefit: a coherent reading experience, additional stories, or a preferred book format. There is no universal free-to-paid ratio. Do not promise bonus material that does not exist.

For serials, choose natural boundaries that preserve scene function and continuity. Establish sustainable backlog and cadence before promising dates. Respect quiet endings; avoid forced cliffhangers and fixed lengths that damage the text. Do not serialize a short standalone work solely to multiply posts.

## Design the return path

Offer one primary next step at each ending: read the next available story, receive a future release, or explore the collection/another available work. State dates only when confirmed. Make series/story title, sequence position, and availability clear.

When an installment is published, update the index and navigation within authorization. Never advertise a nonexistent next story or purchase destination.

## Candidate pilot

For a ready collection without readership or release conflicts:

1. Publish a complete entry story with a concise collection introduction.
2. Distribute representative excerpts linking to the full text.
3. Release the next selected story on a sustainable date and connect reading paths.
4. Evaluate discovery, subscriptions, observed return, and collection interest before allocating more stories.

This is an experiment proposal, not a mandatory calendar. If first-publication opportunities are the priority, prepare a submission route before considering public release.
