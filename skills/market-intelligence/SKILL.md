# Market Intelligence

Build an evidence pack that changes book positioning or marketing decisions. Use fresh sources whenever the request involves current titles, rankings, trends, prices, platform behavior, or audience demand.

## Research frame

Start from the book's genre, language, territories, format, publication stage, promise, themes, reader outcome, and author advantage. If the manuscript or brief exists, inspect it before searching.

Research only what is decision-relevant:

- direct and adjacent comparable titles;
- category conventions, saturation, pricing, covers, blurbs, formats, and release cadence;
- recurring reader praise, complaints, objections, vocabulary, and unmet expectations;
- search/discovery terms and communities where the target reader already gathers;
- credible timing signals and constraints.

Use multiple source types when possible: storefront evidence, publisher/author pages, platform documentation, review aggregates, reader discussions, libraries or bibliographic databases, and advertising/search signals. Respect access rules and do not scrape or reproduce protected review text at scale.

## Evidence discipline

For every material conclusion label:

- `EVIDENCE`: what was observed, with source and date;
- `INFERENCE`: the interpretation and alternatives;
- `SPECULATION`: a prediction requiring a test.

Do not call a book a sales success from rank, review count, or social visibility alone. Do not assume a trend transfers across language or territory. Distinguish author fame, publisher reach, series momentum, price promotion, paid support, and organic demand.

## Deliverable

Return:

1. market definition and reader jobs;
2. 5-12 direct/adjacent comparables with relevance, not imitation instructions;
3. convention map: expected / overused / underserved;
4. reader-language and objection map;
5. positioning territories and one recommended wedge;
6. keyword/category hypotheses;
7. risks, unknowns, and next validation tests;
8. a source table with date and confidence.

Keep facts separate from marketing copy. If reliable current evidence is unavailable, state the gap and design a low-cost validation rather than filling it with intuition.
