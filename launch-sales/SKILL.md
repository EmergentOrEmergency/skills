# Launch Sales

Design the smallest coordinated launch system that can convert existing and earned attention into profitable readers. Support prelaunch, launch, evergreen, relaunch, and backlist modes.

## Diagnose readiness

Check product and measurement before driving traffic: format availability, territory, price and royalty economics, cover, title/subtitle, description, categories/keywords, sample, author page, editorial/review proof, landing links, email capture, tracking, inventory or print lead times, and reporting delay.

Do not modify price, metadata, publishing status, distribution, exclusivity, or storefront content without explicit approval. Verify platform rules and current production requirements from official sources.

## Build the plan

Define objective, reader segment, offer, promise, proof, funnel, channels, assets, owners, dates, dependencies, budget, baseline, primary metric, guardrails, and contingency. Work backward from immovable dates and distinguish hard dependencies from nice-to-haves.

Use phases as needed:

- foundation: positioning, product page, tracking, assets, reader capture;
- validation: small message, creative, and offer tests;
- prelaunch: ARC/reviewer process, partnerships, email warm-up, content runway;
- launch window: coordinated owned, earned, and paid activity with daily anomaly checks;
- postlaunch: follow-up, compliant review request, attribution, evergreen conversion, and experiment closeout.

Never promise reviews or rankings. Free or discounted copies must not require positive coverage. Avoid artificial urgency and ranking manipulation.

## Sales reasoning

Model net economics by format and marketplace, including royalties, printing/delivery costs, refunds, taxes or platform fees when known, advertising, production cost, and reporting lag. Label unknowns and distinguish orders, processed units, KENP, estimated royalties, accrued royalties, and paid royalties.

Return a dated critical path, asset checklist, channel plan, email/content sequence, approval queue, risk register, tracking plan, launch-day runbook, and postmortem template. For live execution, report confirmations from tools; never treat a prepared payload as completed work.
